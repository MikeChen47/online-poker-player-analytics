# Executive Summary

**Online poker players: growth, value, payments and monitoring**
5,028 players who opened an account in February 2015, followed for five and a half years (to June 2020). Data: The Transparency Project, Division on Addiction.

## What we found

**1. The first month decides who stays.**
The number of players active in the last 4 weeks peaks at about 3,250 in week 4 and falls by over 80% within six months. Only 43% of players who started in February 2015 come back in their second month, and about 7% is still playing a year later. Players active in all of their first four weeks are seven times as likely to still be playing at month 12 as players seen in only one week (19% vs 3%).

**2. A small group of players carries the business.**
The top 10% of players bring in 87% of net deposits; the top 1% bring in 37%. The median player is worth EUR 90 over the whole period, the average EUR 1,041.

**3. Losing a player is often temporary.**
A player counts as lost after 4 weeks without playing. Across the period there were 9,899 such losses and 5,007 returns, so about one lost player in two came back. Lapsed players are a real audience for win-back offers.

**4. One deposit in four fails, but players push through.**
Visa, PayPal and Mastercard carry 56% of attempts and succeed only 70-74% of the time; deposits above EUR 500 succeed 58% of the time. Two thirds of failures are recovered the same day, and failures did not make players more likely to leave or lower their long-term value. The cost is friction: about EUR 1.1M of failed attempts were not recovered the same day.

**5. Most alarming weeks are one player, not a trend.**
Of 37 alerts on deposits, net deposits and stakes, 26 were caused by a single player. One failure-rate spike that looked like a PayPal outage was one player making 100+ declined attempts in a week, a risk signal rather than a provider issue. The 2020 lockdown, by contrast, lifted activity in every market (+54% vs the average of the previous 8 weeks) while deposits fell 40%.

## What to do

| Priority | Action | Owner |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Get new players back for a second and third week (onboarding journeys, first-month missions) | CRM |
| 2 | Flag high-engagement and high-deposit new players for VIP contact in their first month | VIP / CRM |
| 3 | Improve card and PayPal routing and decline messages; review limits for deposits over EUR 500 | Payments |
| 4 | Run a win-back program for players 4-12 weeks inactive | CRM / Marketing |
| 5 | Show next to every weekly alert whether one player or many caused it, and route repeated-decline players to risk and safer gambling | BI |

## Caveats

- **No rake or fee data.** Value is measured as net deposits (money deposited minus money withdrawn), an upper bound on revenue: these players' net deposits were EUR 5.2M, while its net loss at the poker tables was EUR 1.1M.
- **One group of players.** Everyone opened an account in February 2015, so the results describe one group's life cycle.
- **Account-level money.** Deposits fund every product on the account, not only poker.
